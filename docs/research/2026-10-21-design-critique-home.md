##### Verdict

The direction is specific to Codraft, and most of it is earned. It is built from the drawing sheet: the frame, the zone letters, the title-block footer with real data, the process axis with its 01–03 bubbles, the audit's grips and the drafted floor plan. The generic defaults are almost all missing: no eyebrows, cards, arrows or monospace, and no numbering on things that aren't a sequence. Three defects need fixing:
- The footer's wordmark breaks its clear-space rule.
- Ten service titles are links but look exactly like the plain titles beside them.
- The layer list looks like checkboxes but does nothing when clicked.

The bigger matter is for the client. The page's two strongest images are the company name, in the hero, and the house, which sells the secondary offer. The main offer (web, software, AI) is carried by the axis alone.

##### First impression (blind)

- **What and for whom:** a studio that plans, builds and maintains websites, software and AI tools for businesses that want scope, deadline and price before work starts, plus a separate line of technical drawing for architecture and construction. Next step: "Pogledajte usluge", or the underlined drafting link if I were an architect.
- **What draws the eye first:** the heavy three-line H1, then the large wordmark on graph paper at the right. The second focal point is the name again; the header shows it a few hundred px away. That is right for the brand but wrong for the offer, because nothing of the work is shown.
- **Memorable:** the drawing sheet (the frame, the column numbers and zone letters in the margins, the title-block footer), the axonometric house with its dark roof, and the central process axis with numbered bubbles.
- **What feels off:**
  - The hero image reads as a logo specimen, close to a placeholder.
  - The house's navy roof is the heaviest thing on the page, and it belongs to the secondary offer.
  - After the axis, the zones are a run of "heading left, content right" splits.
  - The team zone is two empty frames with a gap at the right.
  - Grey placeholder text in places ("Stiže prije objave").

##### Findings

###### DC-01 [Blocking] Brand: the wordmark in the title block lacks its clear space

- **Where:** footer (title block), at every width, most visibly at 375 and 768. Files: `lab-pocetna/375/part-09-footer-title-block.png`, `768/part-09-footer-title-block.png`, `1440/part-09-footer-title-block.png`, `375/frame-08.png`.
- **Seen:** at 375 and 768 the mark has a row of its own. It sits tight against the frame rule above it and the cell rule below it, much tighter than the text in the neighbouring cells. At every width it is 16 px from the frame on the left.
- **Why it matters:** `Assets/codraft-brand/README.md`:40 says: "keep at least the height of the 'c' free on every side of the wordmark". `docs/brand.md` repeats the rule. It is a rule of the final brand package.
- **Suggestion:** in `Footer.astro`, give `.title-block__cell--mark` padding of `var(--space-5)` (24 px) on every side, which is at least 21 px. That costs about 20 px of height at 375. Option B is to shrink `--wordmark-width` to about 6.5rem (104 px, "c" about 16 px) and keep the padding. That stays above the 93 px minimum, but the mark becomes smaller than the header's 116 px, so A is better.
- **Evidence:**
  - The mark cell's padding is `var(--space-3) var(--space-4) 0.875rem`, which is 12 / 16 / 14 px (`Footer.astro`:139).
  - The mark is 8.5rem = 136 px wide (`Footer.astro`:146).
  - In the viewBox `0 0 682.09 147.23` (`Wordmark.astro`:36), the "c" spans y ≈ 42–147, about 105 units, or about 21 px at 136 px. The hero's mark at 1920 confirms the ratio: about 0.15 of the width, approx., read from the screenshot.
  - Clear space is therefore 12 px above, 14 px below and 16 px left, against about 21 px required.
  - The header's mark at 375 is borderline: 16 px from the frame against a "c" of about 18 px (116 px wide, `Header.astro`:92).
- **Confidence:** high

###### DC-02 [Major] Hierarchy and colour: ten service titles are links but give no sign of it at rest

- **Where:**
  - "Što radimo": 7 of its 9 services.
  - "Tehničko crtanje": its 3 rows.
  - At all widths, worst on touch screens. Files: `1440/frame-02.png`, `1440/part-05-tehnicko-crtanje.png`, `375/frame-03.png`, `375/part-05-tehnicko-crtanje.png`.
- **Seen:**
  - The titles are bold navy with no underline. The two stage-01 items, which are not links, look identical to the linked ones.
  - The drafting rows look exactly like the plain titles of "Kako radimo", which are not links.
  - Since #61 removed "Pošaljite skicu", these three titles are the drafting zone's only way forward. Nothing on screen says so.
- **Why it matters:**
  - `docs/brand.md` (Colour, Implications) says "Links are underlined". Because blue isn't the link colour on paper here, the underline is the only signal left.
  - Signifiers (Norman) and NN/g heuristic 4: consistency and standards.
  - On a touch screen the hover grips never appear.
  - This is not a WCAG 1.4.1 failure, because the titles aren't inline in text. It is an affordance failure on the second audience's path.
- **Suggestion:**
  - Option A: underline `.service__link` at rest with the base link style (`base.css`:60–64: 1 px underline, 0.2em offset, 2 px on hover), and keep the grips for hover and focus. Cost: ten underlines add some noise to the axis.
  - Option B: keep the titles plain and add one visible text link per zone in the existing link style, under the axis and under the drafting rows. This needs words from the client (a copy question).
  - A is the minimum for the drafting zone.
- **Evidence:**
  - `ServiceItem.astro`:71–74 (`text-decoration: none`) and :76–79 (underline only on hover).
  - The `metrics.json` targets at 1440 list 7 service links in "Što radimo" and 3 in the drafting zone, 24 px tall each.
- **Confidence:** high

###### DC-03 [Major] Drafting language: the layer list looks like checkboxes but can't be clicked

- **Where:** "Tehničko crtanje", the "Slojevi" list under the drawing, at all widths. Files: `1440/part-05-tehnicko-crtanje.png`, `1920/part-05-tehnicko-crtanje.png`, `375/part-05-tehnicko-crtanje.png`, `1440/motion/scroll-05a.png`.
- **Seen:**
  - Each layer has a 12 px blue square with an inner fill, which is the look of a checked checkbox.
  - In the final state "Osi" and "Kote" appear unchecked, which invites a click to bring the axes and dimensions back.
  - Nothing happens on click. With JavaScript, a real button ("Iscrtaj ponovno") sits on the same row.
- **Why it matters:**
  - A false affordance on the most-looked-at drawing on the page.
  - The drafting page will have a real layer viewer with toggle buttons styled the same way (`docs/motion.md`, "Layers"), so Home would teach the wrong expectation (NN/g heuristic 4).
- **Suggestion:**
  - Option A: make the lamps real toggles (`aria-pressed`), the layer viewer `docs/motion.md` already plans. Visitors could then switch Osi and Kote back on over the house (see Q2). Cost: JavaScript and a decision.
  - Option B: keep them as status only, and draw each lamp as a short sample of the layer's own line, as a CAD layer manager shows linetypes: chain-dashed for Osi, solid ink for Zidovi, the accent for Kote. Mute the label when the layer is off. This carries real information and uses only the drawing's existing strokes.
- **Evidence:**
  - `PlanDrawing.astro`:360–367: an `<ol>` of `<li>` with no control.
  - `PlanDrawing.astro`:624–637: a 0.75rem square with a 1.5 px accent border and an accent fill inset 2 px.
  - `plan-timelapse.ts` has no click handling on the lamps.
- **Confidence:** high

###### DC-04 [Minor] Layout: the team strip leaves an unclosed hole on the right

- **Where:** "Tim", at 1024 and up, most at 1920. Files: `1440/part-07-tim.png`, `1440/frame-06.png`, `1920/part-07-tim.png`.
- **Seen:**
  - The two portraits stop at grid column 10, so a strip of paper sits between the second portrait and the frame.
  - Nothing closes that space: no drawing, no heading and no rule run into it.

##### Verdict

The motion is the most specifically Codraft part of the page. Every movement is a drafting action (a line, a grip, a layer), nothing fades up, the text never waits, and the end states match reduced motion wherever the capture could compare them. What lets it down is choreography, not vocabulary.

The one thing that matters most: around the free audit, four separate drawings start within about 568 px of scroll at 1440, so two or three move at once at the page's single audit call to action. The same happens at the closing band.

Second, the layer row under the house looks like a set of checkboxes and does nothing, and with motion on it changes state by itself.

- **What draws the eye first:** At 1440, the headline, which is right; then the big wordmark on graph paper. At 375 the headline fills the screen and the mark sits at the bottom as a smaller second logo.

##### Findings

###### DC-01 [Major] Motion: at the short zones, two or three drawings run at once

- **Where:**
  - Free audit zone and the boundary into "Tehničko crtanje", at 1440; by zone height also at 1024, 1707 and 1920 (audit zone 400 / 415 / 415 px).
  - The closing band and the title block.
  - Files: `1440/motion/scroll-04a.png`, `scroll-04b.png`, `scroll-05a.png`, `scroll-08a.png`, `scroll-08b.png`.
- **Seen:**
  - **scroll-04a:** two full-width blue selections draw in step (the audit zone's top rule at y≈128 and its bottom rule at y≈541, both at x≈595 after 250 ms), while the audit's step line starts at its first grip.
  - **scroll-04b:** both selections hold with their grips at the frame, and the step line is between grips 2 and 3.
  - **scroll-05a:** the "Tehničko crtanje" selection still holds while the floor plan starts drawing its axes.
  - **scroll-08a/08b:** the closing band's rule and the title block's rule draw together, both at x≈595 after 250 ms.
- **Why it matters:**
  - motion.md principle 2 (docs/motion.md:32): "One moving thing per viewport at a time."
  - A CAD selection means "this object". Two at once reads as a flash, not a selection.
  - The audit zone holds the page's only audit call to action (ADR 0017). It is the place the page should be quietest.
- **Suggestion:**
  - **Option A**, in `draw.ts`, no new tokens: one part moves at a time.
    - A part that triggers while another visible part is still moving waits for it (a selection moves for at most 1020 ms).
    - It is dropped if its rule has left the screen by then.
    - The step line waits for its zone's selection to land (a delay of `--motion-draw`).
  - **Option B**, fewer selections: none on the title block's rule, and none on a zone's bottom rule while its top rule is still selected. This touches ADR 0015's "section transitions", so it is the client's call.
  - Trade-off: A keeps every moment and costs a small queue. B removes moments.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Zone tops (metrics.json, 1440):** audit 2288 (height 413), drafting 2701, closing band 4597 (height 269), title block 4866. Document height 5076, viewport 900.
  - **Triggers:**
    - Zone rules use `data-draw="late"` (rootMargin −30 %; ZoneMark.astro:25, draw.ts:28–31). The audit rule therefore fires at scrollY 1658 and the drafting rule at 2071.
    - The step line (`data-draw="late"`, Steps.astro:27) fires at about 1804. Its top is ≈2434 (approx.), from "1 Forma" at top 2470.
    - The plan (`data-draw="half"`, PlanDrawing.astro:136) fires at about 2226. Its box runs from 2781 to ≈3470 (approx., from the zone padding and "Osi" at top 3439).
    - Four triggers therefore fall within ≈568 px of scroll, 63 % of one screen.
  - **Durations:**
    - A selection moves for 1020 ms and stays on screen for 2180 ms: 700 ms draw, 320 ms grip, release at 1700 ms over 480 ms (ZoneMark.astro:92–105; motion.css:16–23).
    - The steps take 1820 ms (Steps.astro:57–59, 123–152).
  - **What that means for overlap:**
    - At any scroll faster than about 190 px/s (413 px in 2.18 s), both audit selections are on screen together.
    - At any speed above about 70 px/s, the drafting selection is still on screen when the plan starts.
  - **End of the page:**
    - The closing band's rule fires at scrollY 3967.
    - The title block's rule never reaches the 70 % line at 1440 (max scroll 4176 puts it at 690 px, 77 %). It fires only at the page's bottom (draw.ts:89–105), 209 px later.
- **Confidence:** high for what is seen and for the trigger spacing; medium for how often a slow reader meets it.

###### DC-02 [Major] Interaction: the layer lamps look like checkboxes, do nothing, and change by themselves

- **Where:** "Tehničko crtanje", the "Slojevi" row, at every width. Files: `1440/part-05-tehnicko-crtanje.png`, `375/part-05-tehnicko-crtanje.png`, `375/frame-06.png`, `1440/motion/scroll-05a.png`, `scroll-05b.png`, `scroll-06b.png`.
- **Seen:**
  - Each layer has a small square with a blue border and a filled blue inner square, the look of a checked checkbox. In the motion frames it sits beside the bordered "Iscrtaj ponovno" button; the button is hidden under reduced motion.
  - In the final state, "Osi" and "Kote" are empty and the rest are filled.
  - With motion on, the squares fill one by one, then "Osi" and "Kote" empty themselves again.
  - At 375 the row wraps into what reads as a checkbox group.
  - Clicking does nothing: they are list items with no hover state.
- **Why it matters:**
  - This is a false affordance (NN/g heuristic 4, Consistency and standards).
  - The drafting audience knows CAD layer managers, where the lamp is a switch.
  - A control-looking thing that changes on its own, but not when clicked, reads as broken.
  - The client's own words already treat it as a control: "Interaktivni crtež" (batch 8) and "toggle slojeva" (batch 9).
- **Suggestion:**
  - **Option A:** make them real toggles. motion.md:16 already specifies "Toggle buttons styled like a CAD layer manager (`aria-pressed`)" for the drafting page's viewer. On Home this needs a design for what "Osi" and "Kote" show in the 3D view.
  - **Option B:** make them read as status, not controls. For example: no hollow box; the grip square appears only when a layer is on, and the label is muted when it is off. This is a component change and needs the client's word, because batch 9 said "toggle slojeva … ne diraj".
  - Recommendation: B on Home, A on `/tehnicko-crtanje` in phase 3.
- **Evidence:**
  - PlanDrawing.astro:360–367: an `<ol>` of `<li>` with an `aria-hidden` span; no button or input.
  - PlanDrawing.astro:624–637: a 0.75rem square, a 1.5px `--accent` border, and an `::after` fill inset by 2px.
  - metrics.json at 1440: "Osi" is an `li` text block (top 3439), and `targets` lists no layer.
- **Confidence:** high.

###### R-01 [Minor] 7. Motion: when parts trigger in the same frame, a zone's selection can play after its own content

- **Where:** the free-audit zone at 1440. `1440/motion/scroll-04a.png`, `scroll-04b.png`.
- **Seen:**
  - After the jump to 2160, the step line draws first. At 264 ms it shows grip 1 and the line to about x 910, while the audit's top rule (y about 170) is still plain.
  - At 1003 ms the steps are complete. Only then does the selection start, at the left of the top rule (x about 24 to 125, approx., read from the screenshot).
  - So the zone's "this zone" selection comes last. It sweeps the top edge while the eye is on the steps and on "Rezervirajte besplatnu analizu".
  - DC-01's approved option A had the opposite order: "The step line waits for its zone's selection to land."
- **Why it matters:**
  - Every motion should guide attention (`docs/motion.md`, principle 1). A selection that arrives after the content pulls the eye back up instead of leading it in.
  - The queue holds waiting parts "in the order they reached their trigger" (`draw.ts:52`). Each part has its own IntersectionObserver (`draw.ts:65-81`). When several parts reach their trigger in one frame, they start in whatever order the browser calls the observers back, which is not reading order.
- **When it happens:**
  - In a continuous scroll the order is right. The rule triggers at scrollY 1700 (zone top 2330, `metrics.json`; trigger at 630 px), about 146 px before the steps (line at about 2482, approx.).
  - The inversion needs a jump: the capture's instant 540 px scroll, the End key, an anchor, or a fast fling.
- **Suggestion:** inside `draw.ts`, with no new tokens: keep `waiting` in document order (insert using `compareDocumentPosition`). Alternatively, when several parts request in one frame, start the highest one first.
- **Evidence:** `draw.ts:52`, `65-81`, `187-195`. `metrics.json` 1440: audit zone top 2330, "1 Forma" top 2511.
- **Confidence:** high for what is seen. Medium for the cause (the callback order across observers) and for how often real visitors jump.

###### R-02 [Minor, the client's call] 7. Motion: the floor plan plays at double speed for most visitors

- **Where:** "Tehničko crtanje" at 1440. `1440/motion/scroll-05a.png`, `05b`, `06a`, `06b`.
- **Seen:**

  - **06b:** walls and openings are complete.

    - So the plan runs at about twice its speed.
  - **06a/06b:** "Kako radimo"'s selection and its margin letter wait behind the plan.
- **Why it matters:**
  - **The client's decision:** in #117 (Q1, option C) the client recorded "The timing with motion on is unchanged", meaning about 8.3 s to the roof. Only a reader who pauses now sees that.
  - **The queue (#78):**
    - It starts any part that waited at `HURRY = 2` (`draw.ts:47`, `146`).
    - It speeds up a moving part as soon as another part waits for it (`draw.ts:195`).
  - **When the plan waits:**
    - The plan's half-in-view trigger comes about 149 px of scroll after the zone rule's trigger. The rule fires at scrollY 2743 − 630 = 2113. The plan's box is about 2824–3500 (approx., read from `part-05`), so it is half in view at about 2262.
    - The selection moves for 1020 ms (700 + 320 ms, `motion.css:15-17`).
    - Anyone scrolling faster than about 146 px/s there gets the plan at double speed. The house then lands at about 4.2 s instead of 8.3.
  - **The other speed-up:** the plan is also sped up when "Kako radimo"'s rule reaches its trigger (scrollY 3010) while the plan is still on screen.
  - **What the plan waits for:** the zone's selection, the decorative motion of Q2. The decoration takes precedence over the zone's main content motion.
  - **Why it isn't a defect:** it follows the client's own words in #78 ("ako korisnik brže skrola brže su i animacije").
- **Options:**
  - **A:** the plan always plays at full speed. Exempt it from `HURRY` in `draw.ts`, for example with a data attribute. A part queued behind it waits, or is dropped when its rule leaves the screen.
    - Gained: the timing approved in #117.
    - Lost: the next zone's selection is late or skipped more often.
  - **B:** keep it as is, and record in `docs/motion.md` that the plan's length depends on scroll speed.
    - For scrolling visitors the plan lasts about 4.2 s, near the 5 s that Q1's option B aimed for.
    - Lost: most visitors don't see the plan's timing as approved.
- **Evidence:** `draw.ts:47`, `141-146`, `187-195`; `plan-timelapse.ts:37-47`; `motion.css:15-17`; `docs/phases/2-design-review.md` #78 and #117.
- **Confidence:** high for the code path. High that it runs faster than full speed (`06b` against the previous capture). Medium for the exact factor read from the stills.
